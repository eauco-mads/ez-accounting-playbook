The Tax Client Process
  1. Client completes the questionnaire (Personal and/or Business) — at this stage we only ask for a copy of last year's return, nothing else.
  2. EZ Accounting Pro reviews it and sends back a personalized document checklist built for that client's exact situation.
  3. That email includes the ShareFile upload link. Client uploads everything on the checklist there — texts, emails, and Google Docs are not accepted in place of a ShareFile upload unless the client is on the highest-tier plan.
  4. Queue position is not held until every document on the checklist is fully submitted through ShareFile.

Client Service Policies & Expectations Addendum

Effective Jan 1, 2027 — every client signs

Communication & response times

  • Standard 1–2 business days; peak season 2–3 days; after-hours messages answered next business day

Document submission & deadlines

  • 5 business days to respond to requests
  • Monthly bookkeeping docs due by the 10th of each month
  • Tax clients: submit by the stated cutoff (recommend March 15)

Turnaround

  • Bookkeeping: 10–15 business days after all docs received; tax returns completed in order received

Rush / expedited fees

  • $100 minimum, or 25% of the service fee — whichever is greater

Late payment

  • $25 late fee after 7 days; service suspension possible after 14 days past due

Scope & additional work

  • Cleanup, amended returns, extra reporting, advisory — billed at $100–$150/hr, communicated in advance where possible

Professional conduct clause

  • EZ Accounting Pro may discontinue service if communication becomes unprofessional or misaligned with standards

Document requests & follow-up

  • Up to 3 requests for missing info; after that, work may pause
  • Two warnings, then a 3–6 month probationary status requiring full/timely compliance, or possible termination

Payment & account standing

  • No activity/payment for 90 days → possible termination
  • Unresolved balances may go to collections

Service alignment & termination

  • High-touch clients may be moved to a higher tier
  • Either party may terminate with 30 days written notice; balances due before final file transfer

Client Scripts & Difficult-Client Filters

Reference: any team member, anytime

Scripts

When a client is pushy / last-minute
"I totally understand the urgency. Based on our current workflow, we'll be able to complete this within our standard turnaround time. If you'd like to move it up in priority, I can offer an expedited option."
When it's their fault (documents were late)
"At this point, we'll proceed based on our standard timeline since the documents were received after the requested deadline. Let me know if you'd like to explore expedited processing."
When they push back on pricing
"I completely understand wanting to be mindful of costs. Our pricing reflects the level of accuracy, responsiveness, and support we provide. If needed, we can absolutely look at a lower-touch package that better fits your budget."
When they keep asking for extras
"That's a great question — happy to help with that. Since it falls outside the current scope, I can take care of it as an additional request."
Setting a boundary
"To keep everything running smoothly for all clients, we follow a structured workflow and timeline. I'll make sure this is handled as quickly as possible within that."
If you want them gone
"It may make sense to reassess whether this service structure is the best fit for your needs. I'm happy to discuss options or next steps."

November 2026 is the one-time full re-papering — everyone needs a fresh signature on the new engagement letter, the new policy addendum, and (for some clients) a price increase — sent deliberately ahead of tax season, not alongside it. Get this sequence right once, and every future year just runs the standing playbook.

Phase 1 — Aug–Sept 2026, before you send anything

Close the open decisions

Every doc in the Template Library has at least one red "open decision" callout. Lock these first so the letters going out in November 2026 are final, not draft:

  • 1099/W-2 pricing tiers (first 15–20 included, extra beyond that)
  • Account limits — 7 monthly + 3 annual = 10, then $50/account beyond; franchisee limit at 4 accounts / $500
  • ShareFile-only documentation clause — in or out
  • One combined letter vs. separate payroll pricing page, plus the add/remove-payroll amendment doc
  • Consultation flow — pick one path, not two
  • Franchisee letter — separate document or merged with standard
Phase 2 — Oct 2026: test, then Nov 2026: re-sign everyone

Send the new terms well before tax season, not alongside it

Sent in November, not January — clients get real runway to review, sign, or decide not to continue, without that decision getting tangled up in tax-prep messaging or made under time pressure.

  • Oct 2026: test the new letters through the existing ShareFile flow, finalize sequencing
  • Nov 2026: send the new Bookkeeping Engagement Letter + Policy Addendum to every existing client, with a December 15 signing deadline
  • Send the Pricing Increase email to flagged clients (e.g. John David) alongside their updated letter, same send
  • Signatures track automatically through ShareFile; anything unsigned after the Dec 15 deadline gets a December follow-up
Phase 3 — December 2026 → January 2027

Close the loop, then tax season starts clean

December is the last checkpoint before anyone touches tax materials — the January tax list only includes clients who already have the new agreement signed, so no one is ever prepped under an assumption of old terms.

  • December: chase anyone unsigned past Dec 15; send the Year-End Policy Update email
  • January: send the Tax Engagement Letter to every tax client (Jessica flagged this as the biggest gap — make it universal this year) — signed-clients-only list
Phase 4 — Ongoing (2027 onward)

This becomes the standing annual cycle

Once 2027's one-time re-sign is done, future years only need: new clients onboarded through the standard flow (Tab 05), the December Year-End email, and the January standing tax engagement letter push — no full re-papering of the client base again. The November-before-tax-season sequencing repeats every year going forward.

Standard Client Onboarding Flow

The exact sequence a new hire should run for every new client, so nothing (like an 8821) falls through the cracks.

Send the onboarding email

Bookkeeping signups get the Books Onboarding link; tax-only signups get the Tax Client Onboarding link instead. Each form's confirmation page cross-links to the other, so anyone needing both gets caught there. Both forms still need building out (field lists in Tab 03). Engagement letter attached either way.

Track the signature

Log the send date. No work begins until the engagement letter (and Policy Addendum, for 2027 re-signs) is signed and returned.

ShareFile takes over the compliance doc chase

Once signed, the client's onboarding — engagement letter, 8821/W-9/8865, and setup forms — is tracked automatically through ShareFile. A new hire doesn't need to remember to check; they need to know how to read the tracker and act on what it flags.

Set up accounts & confirm tier

Confirm account count against the letter's limit (10 standard / 4 franchisee) and flag anything over that limit for the per-account add-on fee before work starts.

Hand off to standing monthly cycle

Client now enters the Monthly Playbook (Tab 02) at whatever month it currently is — same system every client runs on.

Tab 06

Time Tracking & Invoicing

Built for the real problem: Jessica hops between five client emails an hour, so tracking can't depend on remembering to open an app. This puts the timer inside the work itself.

◆ Tool recommendation

Toggl Track (or Harvest if you'd rather have invoicing generated in the same tool instead of handing hours off to QBO). Either one's browser extension puts a one-click Start/Stop button directly inside Gmail threads and QBO tabs — no separate app to open, no remembering.

Client / Project Setup
  • One tracked "project" per client, named to match the master rollout tracker sheet
  • Set a default billing rate per project — hourly, or "flat retainer, do not bill under X hrs"
  • Rates pull straight from the Pricing Menu already in Tab 03: $100–150/hr cleanup, $150/hr advisory
Daily Workflow
  • Click Start on the client email/task she's already opening — extension button lives right there
  • Opening a different client's email prompts a stop/start automatically
  • Off-email work (calls, QBO) — 2-second manual start from the desktop widget
Weekly — 15 minutes, e.g. every Friday

Reconcile the log

  • Review the week's tracked time, round to the nearest 15 minutes
  • Catch and correct anything mislabeled or missing before it's forgotten
  • Flag any client approaching or over their included hours for that tier
Monthly — 1st of the month

Invoice

  • Pull the prior month's hours per client from the time tool
  • Bill flat retainer + any overage, or straight hourly depending on tier
  • Cross-check against outstanding balances — trigger the Late Payment Policy already in the Policy Addendum ($25 fee after 7 days, service pause after 14) on anything past due
Ongoing payoff

Feeds the annual price review

Once ~90 days of clean tracked data exists, it becomes obvious which clients are under-billed relative to actual hours spent — direct input into the annual price-review clause already in the Policy Addendum, instead of guessing at who needs an increase.

⚙ Sequencing this with everything else
  • ShareFile already handles onboarding docs + signature tracking — this doesn't touch that
  • Once time tracking is feeding clean monthly data, connect it to ShareFile + QBO + the master tracker sheet with a light integration layer (Zapier or Make.com) — these tools won't talk to each other natively, so this is glue, not a rebuild
  • Rush-fee / late-fee auto-triggers (already flagged for May 2027 in the Monthly Playbook) get much easier once hours and invoices are both flowing through one system
